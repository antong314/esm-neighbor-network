Our Collective Why

Why did we come here?

The product of many interviews and conversations. An evolving document we continue to discuss, explore and refine together — a starting point, not a definition.

Because…

We believe it's possible to live a more beautiful life through deeper connection with ourselves, each other and nature. So we're all taking the leap.

Our vision of community doesn't sacrifice individuality — it welcomes each person's needs and journey with compassion. And we aim to create a space where each of us can step into our fullness and gifts.

We know that we are all one and we are all nature. Not separate from it. And so we do our best to respect and nurture all forms of life. And in doing that, we respect and nurture ourselves.

We know that diversity is essential here to the web of life and to community. It strengthens us.

We trust in this exciting experiment, and are eager to learn in the process, even though we know it won't always be easy.

We seek to continue evolving and expanding on a personal level. And we recognize that positive change in our world begins with transformation in ourselves. In fact, the inner and outer change are beautifully intertwined.

We know this community only works if we hold each other in the highest regard. And we know those kinds of nourishing, caring relationships take deep work — on our styles of communicating and on the invisible structures we carry with us. And we're up for it.

We've all sensed an invitation to be part of this community and we want to bring wellbeing to this place and its people.

We know this work has begun. And we aim to be a new step on an ancient path. And with humility and playfulness, we just might have the potential to inspire others all over the world.

And finally, we've come here because we believe in asking questions, like:

What if radical change also involves beauty, joy and celebration?
What if — instead of fear — we choose to respond with love?
Instead of waiting for perfection, what if this is good enough to get going?

Welcome to something rare

Creating genuine community — where people feel known, trust runs deep, and life together feels alive — rarely happens by accident. Most communities that achieve it rely on a shared religion, a charismatic leader, or highly selective membership. We're not doing any of those things. What we're building instead is more open, more resilient and more ambitious: a culture that supports people in showing up as their full selves, because the conditions make it natural.

The Five Commitments

Invitations to practice, not rules

They aren't rules, and they aren't a standard to hold anyone else to. Nobody enforces them and nobody signs anything. They're a shared orientation we opt into together, knowing they'll evolve as we do. You start wherever you are, and your relationship to each one deepens over time. Each commitment comes with practices you can try today.

01

I take responsibility for my experience

I am whole and capable of navigating the full range of what life brings me — and so is everyone around me. When I accept this invitation, I stop waiting for circumstances or other people to change before I can feel okay. I ask instead: what is my part in this, and what genuinely serves in this moment?

This isn't about blame. It's a shift from a passive relationship with my experience ("this happened to me") to an active one ("what is this revealing and inviting me into?"). It also means trusting that others can do the same — not fixing them, not managing them, but giving them the dignity of that same capacity.

02

I stay curious

Growth happens in the gap between what I know and what I don't yet understand. When I accept this invitation, I notice when I've closed down around an opinion, a story about someone, or a way things "should" be — and I choose to open up instead, at least enough to ask what else might be true.

Curiosity doesn't mean having no views. It means suspending judgment long enough to actually hear what's in front of me. It's the difference between listening to respond and listening to understand — and it applies inward too. When something rattles me, that's often the most interesting place to look.

Start here: The Witness SeatThe Opposite StoryThe Ally MapThe Creator's Questions
03

I practice appreciation and generosity

There is more going well than I typically notice. When I accept this invitation, I consciously reorient toward the gifts already present — in this moment, in these people, in this place — not as a way of bypassing real challenges, but as a way of meeting them from a more resourced, effective and creative place.

When my giving comes from authentic presence rather than from trying to secure safety, approval or a particular outcome, I give in ways that genuinely enrich my own life and the community. This orientation minimizes entitlement, builds trust, and makes even the hard conversations easier. It's also contagious.

Start here: The Gift CycleThe Unsaid AppreciationLocating Yourself
04

I address tension directly and respectfully

Tension is information, not a problem. When I accept this invitation, I bring what feels off in a relationship or situation directly to the person involved — honestly, and with care. I don't build a case with others before I've spoken to the person themselves, and I don't carry someone else's grievance on their behalf.

Most relational damage in communities doesn't come from conflict itself — it comes from conflict avoided, or redirected sideways. Going directly to the source is harder, often more vulnerable, and it works. I also take on the practice of being a safe place for others to come to.

Start here: The ClearingThe TranslationThe Ally Map
05

I remember we are experimenting

I am part of something that hasn't been done here before, with all the uncertainty and aliveness that brings. When I accept this invitation, I hold outcomes lightly, treat failure as information, and stay curious about what unexpected results might be showing me, even when things don't go as planned.

Rules and rigid structures feel safe — but they tend to preserve old patterns rather than create new ones. A trust-based, relational culture asks that I deal honestly with what's actually happening and keep orienting toward what's possible. These commitments themselves are an experiment. This is an open draft, and everyone who lives here holds the pen.

Design principles for community culture

Seven both/and principles

One of the most ambitious aspects of ESM is the intentional design of its culture. Using data from interviews and conversations, the Collective Why, and research from many aspiring regenerative communities, neighbors proposed these principles to inform governance and conflict navigation. Each one names two aspects of wisdom that can seem opposed but are actually complementary. Our aspiration is a culture that doesn't just balance but integrates multiple perspectives.

Sovereignty and Interdependence

Each person has the full freedom to make the choices they deem fit for themselves and their families. Full control and responsibility to decisions and outcomes should always belong to each individual.

and

We are a collective where each person's actions directly affect others and the community must be considered in every decision. A community is more than a collection of individuals.

Comfort and Growth

Safety, ease and rest are essential to life. We need downtime where we feel content and supported to recharge and remember the fundamental well being that is available to us.

and

Growth is rarely comfortable. As we become fuller versions of ourselves it can require molting out of old patterns, which can range from disorienting to painful. We embrace the intensity of these sensations because of our conscious decision to grow through and with community.

Inclusivity and Efficiency

Every voice matters. We value the perspective of each person and understand that decisions and conclusions are stronger the more people participate in them.

and

In specific situations, some voices sometimes matter more than others. Expertise, context and commitment are important differentiators. Coordination friction is real, and a few informed opinions are much easier to act on than everybody's opinions.

Learning and Doing

We are so information rich, we should take advantage of the many resources to research, plan, design and consider the best paths forward. Deliberate learning can save significant time and resources.

and

Experience is the best teacher. In an information-rich environment we can fall victim to analysis paralysis. There is a momentum in doing that generates energy and creativity that only comes from action.

Belonging and Diversity

Living in a community with shared values is what draws people to the project. Sharing a sense of mutual purpose and connection with others allows for rapid trust and depth of sharing not possible in the average neighborhood.

and

All walks of life and life experience are welcome. Recognizing the wisdom of each perspective without judgment or conditions creates a more robust and resilient social fabric.

Clarity and Emergence

There is great power in a clear intention, a transparent process, a well reasoned proposal. Clarity provides shared coherence and fluid communication. Acting with clarity prevents unnecessary blunders and conflicts.

and

The world is unknowable. We are humbled by forces and energies beyond our understanding and make room for them, welcome them, in our decisions and processes. We can make plans without being attached to them. As events unfold we observe and integrate new information and respond with presence and openness.

Ownership and Stewardship

We live in an economic system where property and rights are key pieces of financial well being. Meeting economic needs sometimes means making difficult tradeoffs and choices that are also practical and wise.

and

We aspire towards a world aligned with the abundance of nature where gifts flow freely to needs. As stewards of Earth for a time, not owners of it, we make decisions today with a generational mindset for all life.
